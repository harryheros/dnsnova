# Commercial licensing

DNSNova is free software under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0
(AGPL-3.0). That stays so: the community edition is the whole of DNSNova,
with nothing held back.

## What you can do without paying

Anything the AGPL allows, which includes commercial use:

- run DNSNova for your company, your customers or the public, and charge
  for the service;
- run it unmodified, at any scale, with no obligation beyond keeping its
  license notices;
- modify it, as long as everyone who uses your modified version over the
  network can get its source code under the AGPL (section 13);
- use its filtered service with the ShieldNova list it offers by default:
  ShieldNova allows that use in DNSNova, commercial deployments included.

A company that is happy with those terms never needs a commercial license.
Paying is not about being a business; it is about wanting other terms.

## When a commercial license helps

A commercial license grants DNSNova under terms other than the AGPL, for
example to:

- run a modified DNSNova as a service without publishing the changes;
- build DNSNova, or parts of it, into a product distributed under other
  terms (an appliance, firmware, a closed-source platform);
- satisfy a legal or procurement policy that does not accept the AGPL.

It can come with support: response times, help with deployment and large
fleets, security fixes ahead of public release, and features built for
you.

## Why this is possible

Commercial terms can only be offered for code the maintainer may license
that way. Contributions are therefore accepted under a contributor license
agreement (CLA.md) that allows it, while every contribution also stays in
the AGPL community edition. See CONTRIBUTING.md.
